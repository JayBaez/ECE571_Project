# Video Recording Guide

## Before Recording

- Close unnecessary applications (email, chat apps, notifications) — anything that could pop up mid-recording
- Test your microphone — record 10 seconds, play it back, check for clarity and background noise
- Test screen resolution — make sure `PV_ML_Final_Presentation.pptx` fills the screen cleanly in presenter view or full-screen mode
- Open the presentation and confirm it opens directly to Slide 1
- Have the repository open in a second window ONLY if you plan to show code — not required for this presentation, but available if a question calls for it
- Verify all figures render correctly one more time by clicking through the deck once before recording
- Have `presentation/SPEAKER_SCRIPT.md` and `presentation/PRESENTATION_CHEAT_SHEET.md` open on a second monitor or printed, for quick reference — not to read verbatim

## During Recording

- Speak naturally — use the script as a guide, not a word-for-word read
- Do not read every word off the slides — the slides are visual support, not your script
- When a figure appears (confusion matrix, transfer curve, etc.), actually point to or reference the specific part you're discussing — "you can see here that Partly Cloudy gets confused with both other classes"
- Explain the most interesting finding (Slide 10, Problem 5's negative-transfer story) with real energy — it's the strongest "process" story in the whole presentation
- Watch your pace on Slides 7 and 10 (Problem 2 and Problem 5) — these are allotted the most time (1:15 each) and carry the most important results
- If you go long on one slide, you can trim Slide 2 or Slide 5 on the fly — both are supporting context, not results
- Stay within 8–12 minutes total — the script targets ~13 minutes at a natural pace, so a slightly brisk delivery will land you in range

## Final Check

- [ ] Audio is clear and audible throughout — no clipping, no background noise
- [ ] Screen/slides are readable at the recording resolution (text isn't too small once compressed for video)
- [ ] No private information visible anywhere (email addresses, file paths with personal names, browser bookmarks bar, desktop icons)
- [ ] No terminal commands or file paths exposing personal information, if you show any code/terminal
- [ ] Video length is within the 8–12 minute requirement
- [ ] The presentation is understandable without you having to explain the format afterward — watch it back once yourself before submitting
